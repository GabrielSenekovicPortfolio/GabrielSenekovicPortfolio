    <h2>Description</h2>
    <div>Yarn Knight is a twin-stick shooter arcade roguelike where you play as Charlie, a feline traveller from a far away land. Yanzhèn, who can turn into a pink gun that shoots yarnballs, accompanies him on a journey that will take him across 13 different lands where he must convene with their elements and stop a prophecy that has predicted the end of Torusworld.</div>
    <br><br>
    <h2>Gameplay</h2>
    <div>The game is a twin-stick shooter in the vein of The Binding of Isaac, but the levels work completely different. Once you start your run, you are tossed into a randomly selected world and must fight waves of enemies right away. When every enemy is defeated, each exit of the room gets its own directional arrow, which tells you what you can find in that direction, such as another wave, an event or a shop.
    <br><br>
    Since worlds aren't ordered in terms of difficulty, difficulty instead scales with enemy modifiers, enemy selection and attack patterns as you progress. Each world also slowly gets more levels, and once all worlds have been visited they cycle. That means you can go on for as long as you wish, and try to beat your last highscore, but you're gonna have to return to your homebase every once in a while to progress the story.
    </div>
    <br><br>
    <h2>The Home base</h2>
    <div>Charlie and Yanzhèn have their own home base, where they can be visited by NPCs who might set up new areas for Charlie to interact with, such as a garden to plant ingredients and a personal fishing lake. You start the game here, and return here after leaving or dying each run. In order to go on a new run, the homebase has a blue portal.
    <br><br>
    Every once in a while, an NPC might visit your homebase between runs that have something important to say, triggering a cutscene. This is how the story progresses. You will get new quests and motivations to try new things in your next run, in order to bring the elements together.
    </div>
    <br><br>
    <h2>The Story</h2>
    <div>Torusworld is a donutshaped planet, whose moon ping-pongs through the hole in the middle. That's where their God lives, who decides who in the Pantheon gets to live or die. An important coven of witches foresaw that the Moon God would inadvertedly bring about the Apocalypse, and that the 13 elements must be brought together and brought to the moon in order to stop him!
    <br><br>
    Charlie rose to the call for aid, and was tasked with the mission to find a legendary elemental gemstone in each nation. They knew this would take a lot of work and a lot of thinking, so they also urged Charlie to seek the help of the Pantheon. After beating each world, Charlie can visit the temple of each elemental God, and seek their aid.
    </div>
<br><br>
    <h2>Background</h2>
    <div>Yarn Knight was a project that started as an entry for a Game Jam in 2023, where I wanted to experiment with programming concepts I hadn’t had a chance to delve deeper into previously, such as lambda functions and switch expressions. I also wanted to try to make a smaller concept than previously that would be easy to “finish” as well as easily expandable in case I so wished.
    <br><br>
    I also looked further into Unity’s shader graph, and made both the vignette “in and out” effect when you win a level, as well as a palette swap shader, the effects of which you can witness as you go between worlds.
    <br><br>
    Having an interest in Binding of Isaac, a twin stick shooter was something I was interested in tackling eventually, so this is my own example of this genre.
    </div>
